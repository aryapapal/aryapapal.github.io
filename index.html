Research Experience

WALOP Polarimeter Calibration

Inter-University Centre for Astronomy and Astrophysics (IUCAA), Pune
Advisor: Dr. A.N. Ramaprakash (anr@iucaa.in) | July 2025 - Present
  • Commissioning and validating the WALOP-South and WALOP-North four-channel imaging polarimeters, focusing on calibration accuracy, Stokes I/Q/U recovery, polarization-angle zero point, and efficiency tracking for survey-grade stellar polarimetry.
  • Building end-to-end Python pipelines that implement and stress-test the published WALOP calibration model on laboratory datasets, from dual-beam flux extraction and demodulation through to calibrated Stokes parameters and propagated uncertainties.
  • Characterizing instrumental polarization signatures, beam-mismatch proxies, and intensity-to-polarization leakage mechanisms while iteratively refining models against measurements to reduce systematic residuals toward survey tolerances.
  • Proposed (in process) a new Fourier-demodulation-based calibration framework and translated this Fourier-based, Mueller-matrix–driven formalism to WALOP’s four-camera geometry and designed a revised, high-angle-density lab campaign to constrain the new model, in parallel with hands-on work on WALOP-North’s optical setup, alignment, and lab characterization.

    • Rough calibration pipeline

CMB Dust Foreground Modeling

University of Cincinnati, Cincinnati, OH
Advisor: Dr. Colin Bischoff (bischocn@ucmail.uc.edu) | January 2024 - April 2025
  • Developed sophisticated Python algorithms implementing the moments expansion method to model spatial variability in CMB dust spectral indices across the sky.
  • Modeled polarized dust foreground spatial and spectral properties affecting B-mode polarization measurements that are critical for detecting primordial gravitational waves.
  • Analyzed the impact of spatial spectral variation on B-mode power spectra with direct relevance to the BICEP/Keck experimental collaboration searching for evidence of cosmic inflation.
  • Prepared a comprehensive framework for future integration with Bayesian parameter inference techniques and likelihood analysis pipelines on real CMB observational data.

Protoplanetary Disk Simulations

Carnegie Institution for Science (EPL), Washington DC
Advisor: Dr. Dana Anderson (danderso@usna.edu) | May 2023 - August 2023
  • Conducted comprehensive analysis of protoplanetary disk chemistry and evolution by running sophisticated computational simulations to investigate the impact of varied physical parameters on column densities and fluxes of key molecular components.
  • Simulated the chemical evolution of planet-forming materials using the rac2d radiative transfer code and advanced thermochemical modeling techniques.
  • Performed detailed parameterized analysis on protoplanetary disk models to systematically evaluate composition variations and molecular flux distributions under different physical conditions.
  • Presented scientific insights through data visualizations and analysis, contributing to understanding of planetary formation processes and initial conditions for exoplanet atmospheres.
